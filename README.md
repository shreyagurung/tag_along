# Tag Along Website

A website for Tag Along, a hostel and community space in Gangtok, Sikkim.

The project is a redesign of how Tag Along presents itself online. The aim is not simply to list rooms and prices, but to give people a clearer sense of what Tag Along is, what they can do there, and how they can experience Gangtok through the people and connections around the space.

The website is being built around two basic reasons someone would visit:

- to **book a stay**
- to **understand what Tag Along is and what they can experience there**

That distinction shaped the structure of the site.

## What Tag Along is

Tag Along has grown beyond being only a place to sleep while travelling through Sikkim. It brings together travellers, locals, artists, food, conversations, activities and access to people and places across Sikkim.

The website therefore treats the hostel, The Travel Cafe, experiences and stories as parts of the same place rather than as completely separate products.

Tag Along 2.0 is positioned around longer stays, workations and a more lived-in rhythm. The original Tag Along space has a more social hostel character. The Travel Cafe is part of the shared space where travellers and locals meet.

The long-stay offering includes dorms, private rooms, work-friendly spaces, meals and access to the everyday life of the hostel. The existing long-stay material includes weekly, fortnightly, three-week and monthly options, along with separate meal plans. 

## Who the website is for

The site is primarily for people considering a stay in Gangtok, including:

- travellers looking for a place to stay
- people considering a longer stay or workation
- people who want to explore Sikkim beyond the usual tourist route
- people interested in local experiences
- artists and facilitators looking to collaborate or host something
- people looking for The Travel Cafe, its food and activities

It is also for people who may not yet know exactly what they want to do in Gangtok.

Instead of assuming that every visitor wants the same itinerary, the site gives them different ways into the place.

## The structure

The main sections are organised around the actual things Tag Along wants people to know about:

- **Home**
- **Experiences**
- **Host**
- **Cafe**
- **Gallery**
- **About**
- **Contact**

The homepage acts as an entry point rather than trying to contain every piece of information.

From there, visitors can move towards a stay, an experience, the cafe, photographs, or the story behind Tag Along.

### Host

The Host section covers the two Tag Along spaces and their different characters.

Tag Along 2.0 is the more long-stay-oriented space. The other hostel is positioned as the more social stay.

The long-stay information includes room types, rates, mountain views, common spaces, co-working areas, pets, meals and activities. The source material describes everything from ground-floor mixed dorms to balcony mountain-view dorms and deluxe private rooms, as well as meal plans and hostel activities such as hikes, potlucks, badminton, bonfires, karaoke, picnics, BBQs, gardening and board games. 

### Experiences

Experiences are deliberately not treated as a conventional list of tour packages.

The idea is to show things that people can experience through Tag Along, while giving each one some context and a sense of the people and stories behind it.

Examples discussed for the site include:

- day hikes
- monastery visits
- cycling
- food experiences
- rock climbing
- a day in Gangtok
- trips further into Sikkim
- local connections and activities

The experience section is intended to work more like a collection of stories and possibilities than a catalogue of products. Filters can help people find experiences depending on whether they want to explore or simply spend time around Gangtok.

This is important to the project because Tag Along's value is partly in knowing people and places that are difficult to discover through a standard tourist itinerary. The original website discussions described the site as a "door" into those connections rather than only a booking interface. 

### Cafe

The Travel Cafe has its own section because it is part of Tag Along's identity rather than simply an amenity for hostel guests.

The section brings together:

- food and menu information
- cafe culture
- events
- workshops and performances
- the relationship between the cafe, travellers and locals

### Gallery

The gallery provides a visual record of Tag Along.

Rather than using photographs only as decoration around text, the gallery gives the spaces, people, food and everyday life at Tag Along room to speak for themselves.

## Design approach

The design is intentionally more editorial and personal than a conventional hostel booking website.

The project avoids treating Tag Along as a polished hotel brand. The visual direction comes from the actual character of the place: lived-in spaces, mountains, books, food, plants, pets, people, balconies, workspaces and the slightly unpredictable nature of hostel life.

The website uses photography as an important part of the storytelling rather than relying on stock imagery or generic travel visuals.

The tone also matters. The writing is meant to sound like people who actually know Gangtok and Tag Along, rather than tourism copy. The editorial standard for the project explicitly favours specificity, plain English and lived-in writing over marketing language and exaggerated claims.

## A different way of presenting travel

One of the main ideas behind the project is that Tag Along should not simply tell visitors what to book.

Someone might arrive wanting to hike.

Someone else might want to work for a month.

Someone might just want a quiet place, good food and a book.

Someone might want to meet people or find a local artist to collaborate with.

The website therefore leaves room for different ways of being at Tag Along.

The experience section especially is designed around this idea. The team discussed documenting actual experiences through short stories, photographs and personal accounts instead of turning every possible activity into a fixed package. 

## Content and local information

The site also provides information that helps people navigate Gangtok and Sikkim.

The project has a curated list of places including monasteries, hikes, viewpoints, cafes, food spots, cycling routes and destinations across East, North, South and West Sikkim.

Examples include Enchey Monastery, Ranka Monastery, Rumtek, Tinjurey, Radio Dara, Tsomgo Lake, Dzongu, Temi Tea Garden, Ravangla, Yuksom, Khecheopalri Lake and Barsey. Some entries still require confirmation before being published because the original handwritten source material was unclear. 

This is part of the larger idea that Tag Along can help people understand where to go and who to connect with, rather than functioning only as a place to sleep.

## Distinctive interactions

The website uses interaction to support exploration rather than to add animation for its own sake.

The experience section is intended to support filtering so that visitors can find things based on the kind of time they want to have in Gangtok.

Photography and gallery content can be explored visually rather than being presented only as a static grid.

The broader design direction also leaves space for stories, photographs and contributions from people connected to Tag Along. This matters because the community itself is part of the material of the website.

## Booking

Booking remains one of the main purposes of the website.

The site provides clear routes from the hostel sections towards booking or enquiry rather than making visitors search for booking information after reading through the entire site.

For Tag Along 2.0 in particular, the long-stay information makes the different stay periods and room types visible. The existing material includes monthly options starting at Rs 12,000 for the ground-floor mixed dorm, with higher-priced options for mountain-view dorms and private rooms. Meal plans are presented separately. 

## Current technology

The website is being developed as a modern web application using:

- React
- Vite
- Tailwind CSS
- GitHub for version control
- Vercel for deployment

The project was initially developed with Lovable and is being worked on and refined through the local development workflow as the site evolves.

The codebase is maintained as a normal development project rather than treating the generated site as a finished one-off export.

## Project status

This project is still being developed.

The structure, visual direction and major sections have been worked through, but some content and functionality are still being refined. In particular, the experience and story content depends on collecting and editing real material from people connected to Tag Along.

Some local recommendations also need confirmation before they should be treated as final website content.

The website should therefore be understood as an active build rather than a finished representation of every part of Tag Along.

## Looking back

The useful shift in this project was realising that the website did not need to become a bigger version of a hostel booking page.

Tag Along already has rooms, a cafe, events and activities. Those things were never the difficult part to list.

The harder part was explaining why people come back, why some people stay for weeks, how the place connects travellers with locals, and how much of the Tag Along experience comes from things that cannot be packaged neatly.

That is why the website is structured less like a catalogue and more like an entry point.

A person can come to book a bed.

Or they can come looking for a place to spend a month.

Or they can simply come to understand what is happening in Gangtok.

The site needs to make room for all three.
